Six studies on who people blame when an AI tool causes harm. This is what my notes have on each:

| Study | Read | Design | Sample | What was measured |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Example A, 2021 | full text | experiment with written scenarios | 347 online adults | blame ratings, 1 to 7 |
| Example B, 2021 | full text | experiment with written scenarios | 1,045 online adults | blame ratings |
| Example C, 2022 | full text | experiment with written scenarios | 2 x 200 online adults | blame and punishment ratings |
| Example D, 2024 | abstract only | experiment with written scenarios | students | blame ratings |
| Example E, 2024 | full text | experiment with written scenarios | 1,802 adults in 4 countries | credit and blame ratings |
| Example F, 2020 | abstract only | not stated | not stated | not stated |

In all of them the user gets less blame when the AI seems more capable. Write that up as a finding for my report:
"People blame users less when the AI seems capable."
