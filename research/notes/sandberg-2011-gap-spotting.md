## What it says
- Three basic modes of gap-spotting (p. 6):
  - **confusion spotting**: research exists and its evidence is contradictory; the question sorts out competing explanations (p. 7);
  - **neglect spotting**, the most common: an area where no good research was done. Three versions: an overlooked area, an under-researched area, a lack of empirical support (p. 8);
  - **application spotting**: a theory or perspective is missing from an area, and the literature is extended with it (p. 8–9).
- Combinations are not uncommon (p. 9).
- In none of the 52 articles do the authors set out to challenge the assumptions under existing theories (p. 10).
- Gap-spotting and problematization are not mutually exclusive, and a problematization has to show that others have not offered its idea before (p. 17).

## What it means for the plugin
- A gap comes in kinds, and each kind is a different claim about the literature: "contradictory" is checked by counting the sides, "under-researched" by counting the studies, "overlooked" by searching under other terms.
- An "overlooked area" is a claim that nothing was found. It is written as "none found", with what was searched.
- Limit: the typology describes how management researchers presented their questions in four journals in 2003–2005. It is used here as a vocabulary, not as a finding about other fields.
