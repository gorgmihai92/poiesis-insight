## What it says
- A research gap is described in two parts: **where** the evidence falls short, by the PICOS elements that are not adequately addressed (population, intervention, comparison, outcomes, setting), and **why**, by the reason that most precludes a conclusion (Results, step 4).
- Four reasons (Results, step 4):
  - **A. insufficient or imprecise information**: no studies, few studies, samples too small, or an estimate too imprecise to give a direction;
  - **B. biased information**: the studies' methods or designs limit what they show;
  - **C. inconsistency or unknown consistency**: results point different ways, or there is only one study;
  - **D. not the right information**: results do not apply to the population or setting of interest, the important outcomes were not measured, or follow-up was too short.
- Gaps that do not fit PICOS are written in free text. In the pilot these were about 2 of 14.75 gaps per report, on prevalence and incidence (Results, step 5).
- In the pilot the two users chose different reasons when only one study existed (A or C), and counted different numbers of gaps because they worked at different levels of detail. The authors' answer: decide both in advance (Results, step 5; Discussion, limitations).
- Limits the authors state: the framework was applied after the fact to two reports by people who had not written them; whether it finds other gaps than current practice was not evaluated (Discussion, limitations).

## What it means for the plugin
- The `questions` playbook describes each gap by the element that is missing and by one of the four reasons, before any question is written.
- For work outside health care, intervention and comparison become what was varied and what it was compared with. This widening is a product decision, not something the report tested.
- A gap with one study only is recorded the same way every time in a project: the report shows that two people split on it.
- Limit: built for systematic reviews in health care and tried on two reports.
