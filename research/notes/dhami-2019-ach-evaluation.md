# The "analysis of competing hypotheses" in intelligence analysis (Dhami, Belton & Mandel, 2019)

- **Source:** Applied Cognitive Psychology 33(6): 1080–1090 · https://doi.org/10.1002/acp.3550 (pages = PDF pages)
- **File:** `../papers/dhami-2019-analysis-competing-hypotheses.pdf`
- **Licence:** accepted manuscript in a university repository: local only.
- **Evidence:** empirical · 50 intelligence analysts randomly assigned to ACH or not, task with probabilistic
  ground truth (p. 1).

## What it says
- ACH-trained analysts did not follow all the steps; evidence that ACH reduces confirmation bias was mixed; ACH may
  increase inconsistency and error (p. 1).
- More ACH analysts found all 12 relevant evidence items (92% vs 68%) (p. 4).
- Choosing the correct hypothesis: 36% with ACH vs 33% without, no difference; 80% of the ACH group left tied ranks
  vs 19% (p. 6).
- **Analysts who applied their evidence rule consistently were right far more often: 80% vs 31%** (p. 6).

## What it means for the plugin
- Do not impose the ACH matrix as a ritual. Keep what helped: listing the evidence fully and generating
  alternatives. Add what predicted accuracy: one evidence-weighing rule applied the same way to every hypothesis.
- Avoid ties as an outcome: say which explanation is more likely and why, or say the evidence cannot separate them.

## Quotes
- "we observed that ACH may increase judgement inconsistency and error" (p. 1)
