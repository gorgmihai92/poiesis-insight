# The Interpretation of Interaction in Contingency Tables (Simpson, 1951)

- **Source:** Journal of the Royal Statistical Society B 13(2): 238–241 · https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2517-6161.1951.tb00088.x
- **File:** none (reference only)
- **Licence:** © Royal Statistical Society: closed.
- **Evidence:** analytical (worked example) · **abstract only**.

## What it says (from the abstract)
- By an example, the mechanical procedure of splitting a 2 × 2 × 2 table into component tables and testing each is
  not justified even when the second-order interaction vanishes. The reversal of an association between the pooled
  table and its subgroups is now called Simpson's paradox (from general knowledge).

## What it means for the plugin
- A pattern in pooled data is a rival to the same pattern within groups: check it inside the main subgroups before
  reporting it.
