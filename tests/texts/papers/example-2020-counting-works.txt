Counting works in a bibliographic database
A text written for the tests of poiesis-insight. It is not a real source.

A database of scholarly works grows for two reasons. Researchers publish more, and the data-
base itself takes in new kinds of record. The second reason is easy to miss.

[p. 2]
When a database begins to index datasets, the number of works it holds can jump in one year
while the number of articles barely moves. A share computed over all works then falls, even
though nothing changed in the field. We call this the “base effect”.

In our sample of 412 topics, 37 showed a fall in share that disappeared when the base was
limited to articles.

[p. 3]
